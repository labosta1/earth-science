Lesson 2.2 Extra Credit — The Formation and Life Cycles of Celestial Objects

Assignment: Put It In Order: The Life of a Sun-Like Star

These are the stages in the life of a low-mass star like our Sun, listed in the wrong order.

  1. Click Print Blank Copy and number them on paper, or type the numbers on screen.
  2. If you work on screen, use Check My Answers as often as you like.
  3. Write your name and the date on your printed copy.
  4. Submit it using the directions in How to Submit below.

Before you start

Put the stages in order

Write 1 next to the earliest stage, then 2, and so on through 6.

OrderStage
The outer layers drift away as a glowing planetary nebula.
The core runs out of hydrogen, fusion moves outward to a shell, and the star swells and cools into a red giant.
A nebula — a huge cloud of gas and dust — is pulled together by its own gravity.
The star spends most of its life on the main sequence, fusing hydrogen into helium.
What is left behind is a hot but dim white dwarf, with no fusion left in it.
The core passes about 14 million K, nuclear fusion begins, and a star is born.
